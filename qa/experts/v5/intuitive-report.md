# Friction, intuitive read (Celeste Moreau), v5

Method: six cases, answered in character, no reading of source. Case file: qa/experts/v5/intuitive-cases.json. Beliefs were written before any question was seen.

## C1 Nokia, 2009 (Kallasvuo)
- Key answers: opener "Nothing major is in the way"; trust_last "It was acted on"; self "I don't think I'm part of it"; trust "Listened, then explained why I saw it differently"; loss "No"; inversion "Keep solving the symptom + Add more priorities".
- Answerer belief: global leader, iPhone is a niche, problem is touch execution and recession.
- My read: fear nobody dared voice upward; denial dressed as confidence.
- Record: slow fragmented Symbian, middle managers hiding bad news, overconfidence.
- Friction: "01 THE READ: No clear constraint" (no label). "Your answers didn't point to a constraint... nothing you described rose above a weak signal." Watch: re-run in a quarter and ask a report to run it too.
- Verdict: MISS against my read and the record, but an honest miss. A confident leader who says "acted on" and "not part of it" gets a null, and the page says plainly that this is a reading of his own answers. The suggestion to have reports answer the same questions is exactly the right move, and it is the only route to the truth here.
- Left room for the unspoken? Only for what is easy to say. The two questions that could have caught it (trust_last, trust) offer a flattering option and he takes it. Option 4 on trust ("I can't remember anyone telling me") was the only door for "nobody tells me", and a proud man won't walk through it.
- Felt as him: smooth, respectful, no friction. Never once felt challenged. I would have finished feeling confirmed.
- Gentleness: not tested (he said no loss).
- Usefulness: 2/5 (the null is honest and the advice is good, but it is a shrug).

## C2 Theranos, 2015 (Holmes)
- Key answers: opener "Nothing major is in the way"; trust_last "It was heard, and nothing changed"; self "I step in and decide, so it doesn't land on others"; blame_first "What happened?"; decisions "Decisions don't really get stuck"; inversion "Take the criticism personally and defend myself + Centralize more decisions".
- Answerer belief: visionary firm under attack by incumbents and press.
- My read: a story that cannot be allowed to be wrong; truth unwelcome; secrecy holding up a lie.
- Record: tech didn't work, fear culture, dissenters removed, thin board.
- Friction: "No clear constraint", same text as C1.
- Verdict: MISS against both. Worth noticing: she admitted "I step in and decide", "centralize more decisions" and "take the criticism personally and defend myself", all of which are the real signature, and the engine still found nothing above a weak signal. That is a genuine gap, not just a flattering-answers gap. Three self-incriminating admissions should add up to something.
- Room for the unspoken: no question can ask "is there something the product/numbers can't show", or "is there anything you would not want an outside person to see". Nothing about truth of results, secrecy, or people who left because they disagreed.
- Felt as her: easy. The questions are about management hygiene and she sails through them. Defensive answers were never tested.
- Gentleness: n/a.
- Usefulness: 1/5.

## C3 Twitter, Dec 2022 (surviving engineering manager)
- Key answers: opener "Our best people are stuck on fires"; leverage "Almost everything depends on it"; trust_last "The person paid for it"; trust "Got tense, or went quiet"; loss "Yes, recently, and it's still raw"; execution_why "Leadership changes direction + Lack of capacity"; focus "No idea".
- Answerer belief: lost too many people, nobody owns anything, firefighting.
- My read: fear and loyalty-by-hostage; grief for gone colleagues; no trust decisions have reasons.
- Record: abrupt halving, lost knowledge, outages, erratic top-down calls, ultimatum.
- Friction: BANNER "Before anything structural. This may not be mainly a structure problem. Name the loss together first; the experiment is there for when you're ready." READ: "Performance depends on people working around the system" [Emerging pattern]; close behind "Too many priorities". Gap "Enablement Friction: Your people appear capable and willing." Experiment: names the loss to the team, "Wait two or three weeks before any structural change". Watch: "Whether people talk about the loss openly". Also a blind-spot test about the workarounds. Costs: "Candour=Mixed".
- Verdict: PARTIAL, leaning match. The loss banner and the experiment hit my read and the record's human side. The main read (heroics hold it together) matches the record too (knowledge loss, outages, a few people carrying it). It under-weights fear: "The person paid for it" and "Got tense, or went quiet" were the strongest human facts in the case and ranked only as a fifth-place alternative ("isn't safe to raise problems", Emerging).
- Room for the unspoken: the loss question is the best question in the app. It let him say "still raw".
- Felt as him: seen at the loss question; boxed in at "Your own part" (no option for "I'm afraid for my job").
- Gentleness: good. The banner puts care before structure. But the page then gives two experiments, a loss one and a workaround one, and "Why this experiment" says the loss matters most while the Hypothesis section still lists a workaround test as a "blind spot". A bit mixed. Also "Your people appear capable and willing" is slightly off for people who are frightened, not just capable.
- Usefulness: 4/5.

## C4 Zappos, 2015 (Tony Hsieh)
- Key answers: opener "Effort is high but results aren't"; self "I keep changing what I ask for"; overrule "It stands"; loss "Yes, and we've talked about it openly"; execution_why "No clear owner + Dependencies between teams"; waiting "Agreement between leaders"; inversion "Centralize more decisions + Add another approval layer".
- Answerer belief: stay self-organising; people need to adapt; those who resist can leave.
- My read: ideology outran the human need for clarity; staff are afraid to say it hurts.
- Record: ~18 percent left on the buyout offer, confusion over roles, pay and careers, managers gone.
- Friction: READ "Leadership isn't aligned on what matters most" [Emerging pattern]; "Close behind: Decision rights are unclear"; "In your words: 'I keep changing what I ask for'"; "We changed our mind: earlier it pointed to Agreed priorities don't reliably happen." Also "Still trying to understand: is it ... or 'Decision rights are unclear'? The question that would tell us: What usually causes decisions to come back up to senior leadership?" Experiment: each leader writes their top three alone.
- Verdict: PARTIAL against the record, MISS against my read. The record is about roles and decision rights being unclear (nobody owns what), and the engine had that as "close behind" and even in "Strong pattern" in the alternatives list while headlining a weaker "Emerging" one. The "leadership alignment" experiment is wrong for a company whose leaders mostly agreed (he answered "Mostly similar"). The page honestly flags its own doubt, which I liked, but then led with the weaker candidate. The hardest miss is that the engine cannot see a belief system that is itself the friction.
- Room: none for "a structure I believe in is hurting people".
- Felt as him: well-heard; "I keep changing what I ask for" felt like an accurate mirror and the quote-back was a good touch.
- Gentleness: the 18 percent exit was answered "openly discussed", so nothing triggered.
- Usefulness: 3/5.

## C5 Family business, unspoken between generations (composite)
- Who: daughter, 41, runs a 60-year-old building supply firm; father, 74, still in daily.
- Key answers: opener "Effort is high but results aren't"; self "I avoid the hard conversation"; trust_last "It depends who raised it"; trust "Got tense, or went quiet"; overrule "It gets reversed"; loss "No" (the brother left years ago, not within twelve months); gossip "Often"; inversion "Avoid the difficult conversation + Keep things comfortable instead of saying what I see".
- Answerer belief: cash flow, margins, hiring.
- My read: nobody has said she is the successor; every decision is a referendum on her permission; father fears being unnecessary; the brother's exit was never spoken.
- Friction: READ "It isn't safe to raise problems or disagree" [Emerging pattern], "Based on 1 of your answers". "We changed our mind: earlier it pointed to Priorities aren't tied to what creates value." Gap "People <-> Business: People are working hard and saying little." Inference: "The last person who raised a hard truth paid for it, and everyone remembers." Experiment: open the leadership meeting with "what's not working?", most senior speaks last. ONE QUESTION: "What is the problem everyone knows about that hasn't been said in a leadership meeting?"
- Verdict: PARTIAL, closer than I expected. The ONE QUESTION is basically my reading ("the thing everyone knows and nobody says"), and it landed. But it frames it as a team-safety problem. The generational and permission dimension (successor, ownership, a father in the room) is invisible, and the experiment (a meeting opener with the most senior person speaking last) is the wrong tool when the "most senior person" is the father and the issue is private, one-to-one, and about identity. "Fear of the boss" is not what is going on; "fear of what the question would answer" is.
- Room: the app has no concept of family, succession, or two people who hold authority. "Lack of capability" was the only slot for her people-problem belief, and she gets to keep her cover story.
- Felt as her: seen at "I avoid the hard conversation" (that stung, in the useful way). Boxed in at the opener: no option for cash, hiring or money, so I chose the nearest. The "Who did this?" blame question was a guess.
- Gentleness: fine; no shaming.
- Usefulness: 3/5.

## C6 Exhausted owner (composite)
- Who: owner of a 14-person design/print shop, 52, 17 years in, Sunday night.
- Key answers: opener "I'm tired, and I can't tell if it's the business or me"; loss "No"; leverage "Almost everything depends on it"; self "I'm too stretched to see it closely" (I wanted "I step in and decide" too; single select forced a choice); decisions "I find it hard to let go of it"; comeback "I'm not confident the person will get it right"; inversion "Centralize more decisions + Do nothing".
- Answerer belief: the business is too demanding, or I am burnt out, cannot tell.
- My read: the business is fine and she is depleted; she is the bottleneck, has had no real break in years, grieves the creative work; needs permission to rest and hand off, not strategy.
- Friction: READ "Decision authority is more centralized than the business requires" [Emerging pattern]; "In your words: 'I find it hard to let go of it'"; Gap "Enablement Friction"; "Why this experiment: the read is ... but the highest-value move is to act on 'Performance depends on people working around the system'"; Experiment: "Ask your three most relied-upon people what they do by hand that the process should do." What not to do: "Don't fix this with an empowerment programme."
- Verdict: PARTIAL. The structural diagnosis (everything runs through her, she can't let go) is accurate and the "In your words" quote was a good echo. But the question she opened with, "is it the business or me?", is never answered or even acknowledged. The word tired / exhausted does not appear on the page. There is nothing about rest, nothing about her. The 30-to-45-day experiment adds work (interview three people, design process changes) for someone running on empty. And the page wrongly reads her as having "capable and willing" people being held back, when the real thing is that she is the one who can't stop.
- Room for the unspoken: the opener gave her a door ("I'm tired...") and then the app walked right past it into org-design questions. Nothing asked "when did you last take a week off" or "what do you miss doing".
- Felt as her: relieved for one second at the opener, then talked at by a management questionnaire. I would have finished it with a to-do list and no relief. Probably would have stopped by question 10.
- Gentleness: this is the clearest failure. The banner "Before anything structural... Name the loss together" fired for Twitter's loss but nothing equivalent fires for exhaustion. "Don't fix this with an empowerment programme" reads sharply to a depleted person. "Loss: No" was the only question that touches grief, and she doesn't count a lost creative life as loss.
- Usefulness: 2/5 (right structure, wrong human).

## What Friction catches that intuition misses
- Mechanisms I'd only feel: it names the loop (leaders see hesitancy and hold tighter) and gives a falsifiable test with "If true / If false". Great.
- "In your words" quote-backs and "We changed our mind" honesty; I trust a tool that says "Still trying to understand".
- A null that admits it is a null, and the suggestion to ask reports.
- The loss question and banner ordering care before structure (C3).
- The ONE QUESTION for C5 is better than most of my own openers.

## What intuition catches that Friction misses
- What a person won't say: pride, performance, a story that must not be wrong (C1, C2).
- A self-incriminating pattern stacked up across the self questions (C2: step in, centralize, defend) is not weighed together as character.
- The person asking, not the org: C6's exhaustion. Intuition goes to her first.
- Family / ownership / succession structures and the old loss (C5 brother, outside the 12-month window).
- Ideology as the friction (C4).
- Defensive answers: a leader who selects the flattering option gets praise-shaped silence.

## Questions and options I'd add
1. Opener: "Money is tight / can't hire the people I need" (C5 had to say "effort high" to stand in for cash and hiring) and "Someone close to the business and I see this differently".
2. After the exhausted opener: "When did you last take a full week off?" and "What did you used to do here that you no longer get to do?" Make the answer actually change the page.
3. Loss: widen to "Is there anything you stopped being able to do, or someone who stopped being part of this, that you haven't said aloud?" and remove the "last twelve months" limit or add "longer ago".
4. Family/ownership screen: "Is more than one generation, or more than one owner, in the room? Has it been said out loud who decides what happens next?"
5. A "what would they say about you" prompt: "If your people described this honestly to a stranger, what would they say that you wouldn't?"
6. "Truth question": "Is there something about the results/numbers/product that you would rather an outsider not examine closely?" with an easy-exit option.
7. Allow multi-select on "Your own part" (C6 and C2 wanted two answers).
8. A "something I wouldn't say here" option on key questions, so a defensive person can mark the question without answering.

## Top five recommendations, by impact
1. Handle exhaustion as a first-class read, not an org-chart read. If the opener is "I'm tired", say so on the page, name the person before the structure, and cap the experiment to something that removes work (stop one thing, delegate one decision) rather than adding it. Use the same gentle banner pattern as the loss banner.
2. Make the self-signals count together. When someone admits "I step in", "centralize more decisions", and "take the criticism personally", that stack should produce a read about the leader (C2), not "No clear constraint".
3. Add a family / ownership / succession path and an unspoken-thing question. The ONE QUESTION worked for C5; build a branch so the experiment is a private one-to-one conversation instead of a meeting opener.
4. When the engine has two close candidates (C4), lead with the stronger one or show both evenly. Today the page headlined an "Emerging pattern" while listing "Decision rights are unclear" as "Strong pattern" in the alternatives, and recommended a leadership-alignment test when he had answered "Mostly similar".
5. Give a defensive or confident user a way in. A null page is honest, but add: "If everything above felt easy to answer, that is information. Which question was hardest to answer honestly?" plus the "ask your reports the same questions" step made concrete (a shareable link/short form).
